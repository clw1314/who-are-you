Help

What does this page do?

It runs the same sniffers any website can run the moment you open it, then writes down who that makes you: where you probably come from, where you are, what you use and what you like.

Every blank in the paragraph says on hover which sniffers it came from, and the list below shows each sniffer, how it reads (the API or header in the right column) and what it read. None of them shows a permission prompt.

How does it guess my nationality and city?

The city comes from your IP address, looked up in Cloudflare's geolocation database, the same kind of database every ad network uses. With a VPN it's the VPN server's city.

Nationality is a guess from your browser language (zh-TW points to Taiwan, pt-BR to Brazil) and the time zone your system is set to, which a VPN doesn't change. Plain US English is every device's default, so then the time zone decides.

Why can't it tell my age?

Because nothing in the browser says it. Ad networks estimate age and gender from your behavior: which sites you visit, what you watch and buy, often joined across sites by the same fingerprint or cookies. This page reads none of that, so it says so instead of making something up.

What is a browser fingerprint?

Small differences in how your system draws text and shapes (canvas, WebGL), processes sound, which fonts are installed, plus the screen and settings. Hashed together they come out the same on every site, without cookies, in private windows too.

It doesn't depend on your IP, so a VPN doesn't change it. Browsers that protect against it (Brave, Firefox with resistFingerprinting, Safari in private mode) add noise; the "Fingerprint protection" row tells whether yours does.

How do I use the disguise check?

When you use a VPN or proxy or change your user agent, sites compare your readings with each other. A system time zone that doesn't match the IP's, a language from another country, a WebRTC address that isn't the VPN's, a data center network, or a user agent that doesn't match the graphics card all stand out.

To look like someone from the VPN's country, set the system time zone to match, turn off WebRTC or use a browser that keeps it inside the VPN, and use a residential exit.

Do you store any of this?

No. Everything except the first group is read in your browser and stays there. The server only answers one request (/api/me) with what it already sees in that request: your address, Cloudflare's data for it and your headers. It keeps no log, and the code is open source.

Why do some rows say "this browser doesn't say"?

Not every browser has every API. Keyboard layout, battery, connection type, memory and client hints are Chromium only; Safari and Firefox share less, on purpose. A missing reading is a good sign for privacy.
